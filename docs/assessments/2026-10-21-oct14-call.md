## 2. Thinking: Standard or Deep

Long answers were always sent with `reasoning.effort: "low"` (#322, to stop Opus 5.5 spending a whole budget on reasoning). A project can now choose **Deep**: every call it makes, including the draft, checks, chat and Go's planner, sends `effort: "high"`, with 8,192 tokens of headroom beside the answer's own budget.

**Probe (Opus 5.5, a 600-word memo):**

| Effort | Reasoning tokens | Cost | Time | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 71 | $0.033 | 20 s | finished (`stop`) |
| medium | 255 | $0.034 | 27 s | finished (`stop`) |
| high | 555 | $0.041 | 25 s | finished (`stop`) |

**Comparison: three fixed briefs through the real endpoints.** Each brief went through `build-prompt` then `run-iteration`. Each pair was judged blind, in both orders.

| Brief | Standard | Deep | Judge (both orders) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PE add-on memo (≤400 words, invent no figures) | 492 words, 37 s; over the word limit | 426 words, 44 s; within the limit | Deep, Deep |
| Workshop agenda (9:00 → 10:00, keep durations) | 95 words, 17 s; correct | 88 words, 18 s; correct, consistent time format | Deep, Deep (narrow) |
| Executive-search scorecard (evidence only from the profile) | 574 words, 28 s; invented the candidate's gender and overstated the SaaS background | 592 words, 36 s; one small timing assumption | Deep, Deep |

The arithmetic was right on both settings every time; the differences were in **keeping constraints and not inventing details**. Three briefs, one run each: this is indicative, not proof. If Sean's own longer tests agree, Deep could become the default for new projects. Its cost shows in usage as it already does, and nothing else changes.

## 3. The chat-first start

`/projects/new` opens on one box. **Start the conversation** is the main button, and Cmd/Ctrl+Enter does the same.
- The first message is sent at once.
- The outline beside the chat (objective, audience, requirements, facts, deliverables, stages, approvals) can be edited. A part the user edits is kept as written: the backend restores it after every turn (`_keep_edits`), and the model may only add to its lists. What the user typed counts as said, so an added requirement is recorded as theirs, never as "assumed".
- Attachments work in the chat; their text is read with each turn.
- **Make it a project** recommends the setup from the conversation, creates the project, records the outline's requirements and facts, and saves the conversation as the first stage's chat.
- **Review the setup first** still opens the full setup screen. The other ways in sit one row below.

On production (21 Oct) a vendor-choice conversation became a Single output project. Its five facts and requirements were recorded as user-stated, including one typed into the outline, and the four turns of the conversation were on the first stage's chat.

## 4. Timeline and colour

- **Timeline** (next to Read the full work) lists every version of every stage in its version colour, and the decisions between them, newest first. **Open** shows a version on its stage, where Restore already is. It is built from data the page has already loaded; nothing new is stored.
- **Project colour**: each project can take one of the six accents (`projects.accent`), on its page and its card. "Mine" follows the browser-wide colours.
