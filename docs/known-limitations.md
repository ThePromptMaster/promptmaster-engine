# Known limitations register

> Section 12 of the Phase 2 Amendment lists "Documentation and known-limitations
> register are delivered" as a Definition-of-Done item in its own right.

This is that register. It is deliberately honest. A limitation written down is a
decision someone can make; a limitation that surfaces in month three is an argument.

Everything here was verified against the code on this branch, not recalled. Where a
figure could not be verified it says so.

**Status key:** `open` — present and unaddressed. `accepted` — a deliberate trade-off
that should stay understood. `blocked` — cannot be resolved by engineering alone.

---

## Contractual

### L-01 · Administrative access has not been transferred · `blocked`

FR-23's second acceptance criterion — "Repository, deployment, database, provider,
logging, and other agreed administrative access is transferred or confirmed" — **has
not happened**. Repository, Vercel deployments (both projects), the Supabase project,
the OpenRouter account, and all logging access still sit with the developer, not with
PromptMaster LLC.

Section 12 also requires that "Source code and agreed administrative access are
controlled by Moran or PromptMaster LLC."

This is contractual rather than technical, and it is blocked on the client to nominate
the receiving accounts. It is in this register precisely because it is the kind of item
that gets forgotten until it is urgent. **It should be scheduled explicitly, not left
to the end.**

### L-02 · Backup and recovery responsibility is undefined · `open`

Section 9 requires a "Defined backup and recovery responsibility."

What exists: `purge_deleted_projects(interval)` hard-deletes soft-deleted projects
after 30 days, via pg_cron at `'17 3 * * *'` **when pg_cron is present** — the
migration schedules it conditionally and only raises a notice when it is not, so on a
project without pg_cron soft-deleted rows accumulate silently and nothing reports it.

What could **not** be determined read-only from this repository: the Supabase project's
**plan tier**, its **backup retention window**, and whether **point-in-time recovery**
is enabled. `supabase/config.toml` is the local-development config and says nothing
about the hosted project; the only plan-related strings in it are stock comments
generated by `supabase init`.

**Undetermined, and it needs confirming in the Supabase dashboard and writing into
[`runbook.md`](runbook.md).** There is also no `pg_dump` to storage outside Supabase.

## Testing and process

### L-03 · No end-to-end test harness · `resolved`

No Playwright, no Cypress, no browser automation of any kind. Every walkthrough —
including the Book and Research workflow demonstrations and the long-form
interruption-and-resume test that section 12 names — was **captured manually**.

Consequence: nothing automatically caught a regression that only appears when real
components, a real database and a real browser are combined. The two-tab behaviour in
[`saving-and-concurrency.md`](saving-and-concurrency.md) was established by reading
code, not by running two browsers.

**Resolved 2026-09-26:** a Playwright suite (`frontend/e2e/`) runs the real stack —
local Supabase, FastAPI, a production build — with only the model scripted
(`PM_LLM_MODE=mock`). CI runs it on every pull request. Coverage grows per feature;
see [`testing.md`](testing.md#browser-e2e--playwright).

### L-04 · No CI · `resolved`

There is no `.github/` directory and no CI configuration of any kind. `pytest`,
`vitest` and `npm run build` are run by hand, and "the build must pass before pushing"
is a convention with nothing enforcing it.

This is the cheapest item on this list to fix and it protects every other item.

**Resolved 2026-09-26:** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs pytest, lint, type-check,
vitest and the build, then the Playwright suite and every `supabase/tests/*.sql` file
against a freshly migrated local Supabase.

### L-05 · No SQL test harness · `open`

There is **no pgTAP, no `supabase test db` wiring, and no CI step that runs SQL**.

Most schema invariants in [`data-model.md`](data-model.md) are asserted by **grepping
migration text**. That is a real guard against a later edit weakening the DDL, and it
proves the SQL *says* the right thing. It does not prove a trigger *fires*, that an RLS
policy *denies*, or that a composite foreign key *rejects* a cross-user parent.

The one exception is `supabase/tests/fr02_proposal.sql`, which genuinely exercises the
FR-02 proposal trigger. It was run on 2026-09-09 against a throwaway PostgreSQL with
every migration replayed from empty; all 10 assertions passed, **and a negative control
was run** — with the trigger dropped, a pending proposal could be cited and an
`actor='system'` row could cite one, confirming the foreign key alone does not enforce
FR-02. That is the right way to write such a test.

But **running it is a manual act**, and nothing re-runs it. Until a harness exists it
can rot without anything going red.

Nothing exercises the ten `SECURITY DEFINER` job functions against a real Postgres
either; `drain.test.ts` runs against an in-memory fake that models their behaviour.
The fake and the functions can drift, and nothing would notice.

This matters more than usual here because these invariants are being offered as
contract evidence. **The single highest-value fix in this register is a harness that
runs `supabase/tests/*.sql` in CI** — it would convert L-05 and much of L-04 at once.

## Data and schema

### L-06 · Two stage-history tables, one of them unused · `open`

`workflow_events` is live: `projectState` in `lib/workflow/engine.ts` projects stage
state from it, and it is the table the product actually uses.

`project_stage_events` has **zero readers and zero writers** in application code. The
only references anywhere are the migrations and one comment in `lib/workflow/types.ts`
pointing at it.

The more serious half of this — that the *contract evidence* (`proposal_id` and the
FR-02 boundary) sat on the dormant table while the *data* lived on the live one — was
**resolved on 2026-09-09** by `20260909000000_recommendation_governance.sql`, which
adds `proposal_id` to `workflow_events` and installs the
`stage_event_proposal_accepted()` trigger on **both** tables, explicitly "so the rule
cannot hold on one and not the other". See L-10.

What remains is the duplication itself: two tables with the same purpose, the same
actor restriction and now the same trigger, one of which nothing writes.
**A consolidation is still owed** — drop `project_stage_events`, or adopt it — because
leaving both invites someone to write to the wrong one, and every future invariant now
has to be installed twice to stay honest.

### L-07 · 132 chat messages remain deliberately detached · `accepted`

The M1 import found `189 of 339` chat messages joinable and **150 orphaned across 21
session_ids**, caused by two bugs since fixed: the summary phase minted a fresh random
`session_id` when saving while the chat panel used the store's id, and a session was
only persisted on reaching the Summary phase, so a user who chatted and closed the tab
produced messages with no session row at all.

Time proximity was rejected as a recovery method — it yields five or six candidate
projects for most conversations, and a wrong attribution puts someone's conversation on
a stranger's project, which is worse than leaving it detached. **Only content-provable
links were made**: a user message is echoed verbatim into the prompt of the iteration
it produced, so a message found inside exactly one project's version text is a link
rather than a guess. Three conversations cleared that bar and were reattached by
`20260904000300_reattach_provable_chat.sql`; **18 conversations were deliberately left
detached**, 8 of them unrecoverable in principle.

Nothing is lost — every message row is intact with a null `project_id`. But they are
currently **invisible**: no UI surfaces unattached conversations. A later UI could show
them as unattached rather than absent.

*Verification note:* the migrations record `150` orphaned before reattachment and `18`
conversations remaining. The commonly quoted figure of **132 remaining messages** is
consistent with those (150 minus the 18 messages reattached) but **is not itself stated
anywhere in the repository** and was not re-measured against production here.

### L-08 · `decisions.decision_type` has no `defer_recommendation` · `open`

The CHECK constraint allows exactly eight values:

```
'accept_recommendation', 'dismiss_recommendation', 'restore_version',
'skip_stage', 'advance_stage', 'return_stage', 'finalize', 'rate_version'
```

**Deferring a recommendation cannot be recorded**, so it is absent from the
append-only decision trail. Accepting and dismissing are captured; "not now" is not.
Since the trail is the auditable record of how a project reached its state, a decision
type that cannot be written is a hole in it.

Fix is a one-line migration adding the value.

### L-09 · Append-only is enforced by two different mechanisms · `accepted`

`artifact_versions` immutability is a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger permitting only
`user_rating` to change. **It does not block DELETE.** Deletion is prevented instead by
the *absence* of a DELETE policy under RLS — which means a **service-role client
bypasses it entirely**. The drain route holds the service-role key.

Nothing today deletes versions, and `on delete cascade` from `projects` is a
legitimate path the purge function relies on. But "append-only" is a weaker statement
than it sounds: it is append-only *for users*, not for the system.

### L-10 · ~~`proposal_id` does not require an accepted recommendation~~ · `resolved 2026-09-09`

**Resolved.** Previously the `proposal_id` foreign key pointed at `recommendations(id)`
with nothing requiring `status = 'accepted'`, so FR-02's guarantee was only half
schema-enforced.

`20260909000000_recommendation_governance.sql` adds the
`stage_event_proposal_accepted()` trigger, on both history tables, rejecting four
distinct ways the boundary could be claimed without being kept: a proposal that does
not exist, one belonging to another user, one that is not `accepted`, and any row where
`actor <> 'user'` — that last being the exact shape FR-02 forbids, a model owning a
transition with a recommendation stapled on as cover.

Kept in the register rather than deleted, because a resolved item is evidence too.

### L-11 · `conversation_messages.version_id` has no foreign key · `accepted`

Deliberately deferred during the M1 import and still absent. The original
`conversation_messages` table also pointed at sessions by a plain text field with no
foreign key — which is *why* 150 messages could be silently orphaned in the first
place, since nothing in the database could complain. The same shape is still present on
`version_id`.

### L-12 · Re-seeding a published template version is a silent no-op · `accepted`

Generated seed migrations end in `on conflict (key, version) do nothing`. On a database
that already holds `(book, 2)`, a later migration re-inserting `(book, 2)` with a
different definition **keeps the old one and reports nothing**.

This is correct — it is what makes published templates immutable — but it is a trap for
anyone who edits a `.v1.ts` file in place and regenerates. `seed-drift.test.ts` compares
TypeScript to the *migration file*, so it would still pass while production kept the
old definition.

`book` v2 currently appears in two seed migrations (`20260906000100` and
`20260908000000`). **Their definitions are byte-identical**, verified — so there is no
live divergence today. The rule to follow: **bump the version, never edit a published
one in place.**

### L-13 · `supabase/config.toml` references a seed file that does not exist · `open`

`[db.seed] enabled = true`, `sql_paths = ["./seed.sql"]`, and there is no
`supabase/seed.sql`. Cosmetic for now; confusing to a newcomer running Supabase
locally.

## Security and operations

### L-14 · No feature flags · `open`

Section 9 asks for "Feature flags for experimental functionality where practical."
There is **no flag mechanism anywhere** in the codebase. Experimental functionality is
gated by whether the UI happens to call it — see L-15.

### L-15 · The API surface is much larger than the product · `accepted`

Eighteen of twenty-five browser-facing FastAPI endpoints have **no caller** — the
server side of the retired `/session` flow. They remain authenticated and were
deliberately not deleted, so that re-surfacing any of them is a UI job rather than a
rebuild. That was the right call.

But they are still **billable against the OpenRouter key** by any signed-in user, and
there is no per-user rate limit or spend cap (L-16). If the beta widens, they should go
behind a flag (L-14) or be removed.

Inventory in [`api.md`](api.md).

### L-16 · Usage is metered and rate-limited; the hard spend ceiling is the provider's · `resolved 2026-10-04 (register corrected)`

Section 9 asks for "Rate limiting and usage metering appropriate for controlled beta",
and FR-18 for per-user cost controls. This entry said neither existed; both have since
been built, and the register had not caught up.

- **Rate limiting.** `backend/ratelimit.py` is applied to every protected router at
  include time (`main.py`, `_protected`), per user, with `RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE` /
  `RATE_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` and a kill switch. It is an in-process sliding window: a burst
  limiter and circuit breaker against a runaway client, not a billing quota — under
  fan-out to N cold instances a caller gets up to N windows.
- **Metering.** Every model call is recorded in `model_usage` (insert/select only;
  `cost_usd` null means "price unknown"), and `/api/admin/overview` shows it.
  `usage_tracking` / `recordUsage()` are superseded, not extended.
- **The hard ceiling** is the spend cap on the OpenRouter account, plus the per-request
  generation-size caps in `promptmaster/limits.py`. There is no per-user dollar cap in
  the product; `agent_runs.budget_usd` exists and is not enforced (L-B4).

### L-17 · `AUTH_ENFORCED` can disable authentication · `accepted`

`AUTH_ENFORCED=false` lets unauthenticated requests through the FastAPI backend,
logging `UNAUTHENTICATED_REQUEST`. It defaults to `true` and exists as a
deploy-ordering lever. It is a correct piece of rollout machinery and a loaded gun;
confirm it is unset or `true` in production, and alert on that log line.

## Frontend

### L-18 · Stale reads across tabs are never warned about · `open`

There is no realtime subscription and no polling on `projects`. A second tab shows
stale data **indefinitely**, with no indicator, and only discovers the conflict when
the user next edits. "Keep my changes" then asks the user to overwrite without showing
them what they would overwrite — the banner names no fields and shows no diff.

Stale *saves* are reliably prevented, which is what FR-21 requires. Stale *reads* are
not surfaced. Full description in
[`saving-and-concurrency.md`](saving-and-concurrency.md).

### L-19 · ~~The version-append path has no concurrency guard~~ · `resolved`

Resolved by `20260927000200_artifact_version_append.sql`. `version_number`,
`parent_version_id` and the artifact's head (`current_version_id`, `version_count`) are
now set by triggers under a per-artifact advisory lock, the same pattern as
`workflow_events.seq`. Two tabs appending serialise into two versions; history is
append-only, so neither write is lost.

The old path was worse than "unguarded". It numbered from a cached `version_count` and
moved the head with an UPDATE guarded on `revision`, which *every* artifact write bumps,
including the outline's 800 ms draft autosave. An outline edited, left for a second,
then approved wrote v1 but never moved the head, and every retry collided on v1 again:
the project could not get past Outline. The migration also repairs artifacts left in
that state. Test: `supabase/tests/artifact_version_append.sql`.

### L-24 · Stages after drafting are sent the manuscript, bounded · `accepted`

Continuity, Critique, Fact-check and Final review receive the drafted chapters in their
prompt (`digest.manuscript`), capped at `MANUSCRIPT_MAX` (120,000 characters, about 30k
tokens). Past that each chapter is cut to an equal share and the cut is marked. For a
long book those stages therefore review the opening of each chapter, not all of it.
Reviewing chapter by chapter would remove the cap and multiply the calls. The earlier
design sent a 320-character summary, and the review stages reviewed the summary.

### L-25 · A Revision/Editing rewrite keeps the old text as a flat snapshot · `open`

Before a Revision or Editing stage rewrites the chapters, the whole manuscript is saved
as one `manuscript_snapshot` version on the drafting artifact. Nothing is lost, but
there is no one-click restore of a snapshot back into the per-section structure, and no
per-section diff. Restoring means copying text back by hand.

### L-20 · `components/ui/` is largely dead, and carries a second design system · `open`

Eighteen files. **Ten have no importers at all**: `badge`, `dialog`, `dropdown-menu`,
`scroll-area`, `select`, `separator`, `sheet`, `tabs`, `textarea`, `tooltip`.

The eight that are live — `button`, `card`, `input`, `label`, `sonner`, `spotlight`,
`splite`, `container-scroll-animation` — are used only by the marketing page
(`app/page.tsx`), the signup page, the root layout and the error boundary. They carry a
**second token system** (shadcn-style Tailwind tokens), whereas the product surface uses
the "Foundry Slate" CSS custom properties. So the two design languages meet at the
signup page and the error boundary.

*(Commonly quoted as "15 of 20 dead"; the verified figure on this branch is 10 of 18.)*

### L-21 · Five Supabase table modules have no importers · `accepted`

`lib/supabase/sessions.ts`, `templates.ts`, `usage.ts`, `custom-modes.ts` and
`presets.ts` are all unreferenced. This is a known consequence of retiring `/session`,
and `sessions.ts` in particular is deliberately retained as the rollback path for the
M1 import. Noted so that "unused" is not mistaken for "safe to delete" — `sessions`
must not be narrowed or dropped.

### L-22 · `templates` has no RLS UPDATE policy · `accepted`

`saveTemplate()` upserts on `(user_id, template_id)`, and an upsert that hits the
conflict needs UPDATE — so it would fail under RLS. The baseline migration reproduces
live state exactly and deliberately did not fix it. Harmless today because
`saveTemplate` has **zero call sites**; a live bug the moment templates are re-surfaced.

### L-23 · `touch_updated_at()` is defined and never attached · `open`

Dead function in `20260902000000_projects_core.sql`. Cosmetic, but it sits next to
`touch_and_bump_revision()`, and a future editor could easily attach the wrong one —
which would silently stop bumping `revision` and disable the concurrency guard.

### L-26 · Go's planner reads the opening of the manuscript, not all of it · `accepted`

On a stage after Drafting, the planner is sent the chapter list, the counts and the first
6,000 characters of the manuscript (`MANUSCRIPT_EXCERPT_CHARS`, `lib/agent/digest.ts`),
enough to know the draft exists and what it covers. The full text, bounded at 120k (L-24),
goes only to the call that drafts the review. A planner that needs to judge a late chapter
cannot; it can only draft the review, which can. The planner runs on every step, so the
whole manuscript on every call would pay for the same text over and over.
### L-27 · The user's brief has no copy of its own · `accepted`

`projects.objective` is the user's typed brief, and nothing in setup or any stage
rewrites it (the setup suggester returns no objective). But it is editable in place from
the first stage and from the project brief, so an edit replaces the original with no
record of what it was. A separate immutable `brief` column was considered on 2026-10-03
and not added: the drift the client saw came from the first stage's *statement* of the
objective being read as if it governed, which the prompt now says it does not, and from
the brief being easy to overlook, which the page now addresses. If a record of the
original wording is ever needed, it is a nullable column set at creation.

### L-28 · Images: placed by caption, never seen · `accepted`

Since 2026-10-03 a project can hold images (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, up to 5 MB each, in
`project_files` with `preview.kind = 'image'`). A draft places one as
`![caption](project-file:<id>)`. The image's id is stable, so the text does not depend on
a link that expires.

What is not done:
- **No model sees the pixels.** A prompt is told each image's caption and the exact text
  that places it, so placement is only as good as the caption. Vision input would need
  multimodal content in `llm_client` and a cost decision.
- **Chapters cannot be edited by hand.** They get images only when the model places them
  while drafting or revising. Prose stages also have an "Insert image" button in the editor.
- **Exports:**
  - Word embeds PNG, JPEG and GIF. A WebP image appears as a caption, because Word cannot
    hold it.
  - A Markdown export or copy links each image for seven days.
  - PDF (the print page) draws images while the page is open.
- **Captions are fixed at upload,** because `project_files` has no update policy. To
  change a caption, remove the image and add it again.

### L-31 · Exit criteria are enforced by the app, not by the database · `accepted`

What a stage needs before it can be completed (`exit_criteria`, evaluated by pure
functions in `lib/workflow/engine.ts`) is checked in the browser. The database enforces
who may record a stage move (`actor` is `user | system`, a model only through an accepted
recommendation, Go only within a recorded authorization), that cited evidence belongs to
the stage, and that skips and blocks carry a reason — but a `user`-actor
`stage_marked_complete` is accepted whether or not the requirements are met, and the
override-needs-a-reason rule is enforced only in the UI.

For the product as it is, the user *is* the authority, so this is the right side of the
line. It matters for the governance direction discussed on 4 Oct: an external agent
writing through the API with a user's token would bypass the app's checks. Before agents
other than PromptMaster's own may act on a project, the same pure functions need to run
server-side (a route that evaluates them before writing), and an agent needs an identity
of its own rather than the user's (`docs/architecture.md`, § Governance layer).

### L-32 · The event log is read 1,000 rows at a time · `open`

`listWorkflowEvents` reads `workflow_events` in `seq` order with no paging, and the
PostgREST `max_rows` is 1,000 (`supabase/config.toml`). A project with more events than
that would project from the oldest 1,000 and lose its newest state. Stage moves are a few
dozen per project and Go's steps are in `agent_steps`, not here, so no project is near
it; page the read before one is.

### L-33 · Password reset: works on any device; delivery is the open part · `open`

Supabase only redirects to URLs on the project's allow-list (Auth → URL Configuration);
production lists `https://promptmaster-engine.vercel.app/auth/callback` exactly. Since
2026-10-05 the reset email's link is a token-hash link to that callback
(`supabase/templates/recovery.html`, applied to production through the Management API),
which verifies on whatever device opens it and goes to `/auth/reset`. A reset requested
through the code exchange is still handled: the browser that asked remembers it for an
hour (`rememberResetRequest`, `lib/auth/next-path.ts`).

**Delivery is not solved.** Production has no custom SMTP (`smtp_host` is unset), so auth
email goes through Supabase's built-in sender: rate-limited (`rate_limit_email_sent` = 2
per hour) and, by Supabase's policy, delivered only to the project's team members. An
outside beta user asking for a reset would receive nothing. Setting it needs an email
provider account (Resend, Postmark, SendGrid…) and its SMTP credentials under the
company: a decision and an account for the client, then one Management API call.

### L-34 · Research keeps its outline and its manuscript on one row · `accepted`

A derived outline (Research) lives on the drafting stage's outline artifact, and so does
the paper written from it; completing Drafting appends the full report there as a "Full
draft saved" version. Since 2026-10-04 the outline panel reads only outline versions (JSON)
from that row, so the prose snapshot no longer shows as an empty outline. Splitting the
manuscript onto its own artifact would be cleaner and needs a data migration for every
existing Research project; not worth it for the beta.

### L-35 · A literature topic search returns what the index matches, not what is relevant · `accepted`

"Check literature" on a list with no works searches OpenAlex by topic and adds what it
returns as "Retrieved by PromptMaster" rows — a record with that title exists, nothing
more. Some returns are off-topic. Since 2026-10-04 a topic search runs only while the
stage is short of the works its own requirement asks for; after that, Check literature
looks up what is listed. Relevance is the user's to judge (they remove rows that do not
belong); filtering by the index's relevance score or by reading abstracts is not built.

### L-36 · The commit check guards shape, not quality · `accepted`

Since 2026-10-05 every new stage version passes `checkCommit` (`lib/workflow/commit-check.ts`)
inside `appendStageVersion`. A model's revision is refused, and the current version kept,
when it is:
- empty;
- text where the stage holds a table;
- a table that lost every row.

It does not judge whether a revision is *better*, and it does not refuse a table that lost
some of its rows. It does not judge length either: a rule that refused rewrites keeping
under 30% of the text was removed on 2026-10-06, the day it shipped. Findings that ask for
a 23,000-character overshoot to be cut back to a statement are asking for exactly that. Hand edits and restores are exempt, so a user may empty a stage on purpose.

### L-37 · "Figures must have a source" is a lexical check · `accepted`

Since 2026-10-05 a stage check adds a finding for every percentage, amount of money,
magnitude (m, bn, k) or decimal in the draft, ranges included, that does not appear in:
- the project's objective, constraints or context;
- its data files;
- another stage's text.

The check is unit-aware: a percentage needs a percentage, and money needs money. It is
code, not a model. The trade-offs:
- **Written another way:** a figure written differently in its source ($41.2m against
  $41,200,000) is reported.
- **Derived by calculation:** a figure derived by calculation is reported unless a sandbox
  run recorded it in a stage.
- **Plain counts:** plain counts ("three plants", "18 months") are not checked.
- **Assumption labels:** a sentence that labels its figure as an assumption or as
  illustrative passes; "scenario" does not.

### L-38 · Project context is capped at 200,000 characters and rides on every call · `accepted`

`projects.context` (2026-10-05) is the user's source material, kept apart from the
objective. It is capped at 200,000 characters, about 50k tokens (`MAX_CONTEXT_CHARS`;
60,000 until 6 Oct, raised so the text of an attached PDF or Word brief fits).
- **Where it goes:** it is included in every prompt that produces or judges stage work,
  so a long context costs tokens on each of those calls. Go's planner gets only a
  4,000-character excerpt.
- **Not retrieval:** there is no retrieval over it; it is sent whole.
- **Over the cap:** material beyond 200,000 characters belongs in a data file. Today the
  model sees only the first rows of a text file (L-B3), so very large sources are not yet
  usable.
- **The brief fields** (6 Oct): objective, audience, constraints and output format are
  capped at 60,000 characters each, and instructions at 20,000 — abuse ceilings, not
  product limits. Each field shows a counter from 80% of its cap and stops taking text at
  the cap, so a saved field can no longer fail every later stage with a 422.

### L-39 · No dependency graph: staleness is coarse · `open`

When an earlier stage changes:
- its figures stop being handed to later stages (`establishedFigures`);
- a return marks later stages stale.

PromptMaster does not record which claim rests on which evidence. So it cannot say which
specific conclusions, rows or paragraphs a change invalidates, or repair only those. The
design for it is an additive links table (from → to, kind = supports / derived_from /
cites) over the existing ids (`docs/architecture.md` § Project state model). That is the
main foundational piece of the governance direction discussed on 2026-10-04/05.

### L-40 · At an approval, Go asks rather than reasons further · `accepted`

Since 2026-10-05 the boundary is reached when:
- everything still open on a stage is a box only the user ticks;
- the draft is finished, its findings are applied and it has been checked.

At that point Go asks for the tick at once. Reasoning moves (Compare alternatives,
Derive, …) are not taken first, because they cannot satisfy an approval. A user who wants
more reasoning before approving asks for it in the side chat, or answers Go's card with a
direction. Two things still count as work Go may do first:
- an optional automatic item that is still open (Literature's "three works verified");
- a computation the project's data can run.

### L-B3 — Go mode code execution: Python only, fixed package set, no network

`/api/sandbox/run` runs model-written Python 3.13 in a Vercel Sandbox microVM with
numpy, scipy, sympy, matplotlib and (since 2026-10-01) pandas, and **no network** while the code runs. Anything
else — another language, another library, a dataset fetched from the internet —
is recorded as `blocked` / `tool_missing` (or `data_missing` for a missing input
file) rather than attempted another way.

**Data files (2026-10-01).** A user can attach CSV, TSV, JSON or text files to a
project (`project_files` + the private `project-files` bucket; ≤5 MB each, 20 per
project). The sandbox route copies them into `/data` for every run of that project
(≤20 MB in total; files past that are left out, in upload order). Prompts are shown
each file's name, columns, first rows and row count — computed in the browser at
upload — never the file. An Excel workbook (.xlsx) is converted in the browser to one
CSV per sheet that holds anything (`lib/data/spreadsheet.ts`); the CSVs are what is
stored, previewed and run against, and the workbook itself is not kept. Values survive;
formulas arrive as their last computed results, and formatting, charts, merged cells and
the old `.xls` format do not arrive at all. What this does **not** do: query a database.

**Documents (2026-10-06).** PDF, Word (.docx) and Markdown files can be attached too, on
the start screen or on any stage (20 per project). Their text is read in the browser
(`lib/data/extract-text.ts`, pdf.js and mammoth) and goes into the project context —
on the start screen straight away, so the suggested setup and the Guide questions read
it (their calls see the first 12,000 characters); on a stage when the user presses "Add
its text to the project context". A scanned PDF has no text layer and is refused with
that reason: there is no OCR. Tables, images and layout inside a document are not kept,
only its words; the file itself is stored and copied into `/data` like any other.

**A run that executed settles its row (2026-10-01).** When the planner names the row of
the stage's table a computation carries out (`params.row`) and the code exits cleanly,
that row becomes **Completed** with "Recorded from a sandbox run", the run's id and the
first 300 characters of what it printed (`lib/workflow/run-result.ts`), saved as a
version. It is the planner's word that the code *was* that run — nothing checks the code
against the row's description — which is why the row says where its status came from and
stays the user's to change; a row the user decided is never overwritten. With data
attached, Go tries a computation at most once per row before handing an undecided run
table to the user. **Without data (2026-10-09, Q1a)** each row says who can carry it out
(`producible_by`: PromptMaster, or what it needs that the project lacks — data, a
person, a tool). A row PromptMaster can carry out from what the project states is not
settled by the draft calling it "not run": Go runs it, once, and the row records the
output, or the exact reason it could not run (no sandbox, missing data, or the error the
code ended with). That classification is the model's, written in the draft; a row it
misjudges as needing data is still left to the user. A row blocked this way is set aside
and the run goes on with other work (`setAside`), rather than ending. A reason "needs a
tool" that names only computation (integration, simulation, a fit) is read as
PromptMaster's (#203). **Each run has 30 seconds** (`COMMAND_TIMEOUT_MS`): the code-writer
is told so and prints `reduced: …` when it narrows a sweep to fit; a run that still times
out is recorded on its row as not run, with that reason, and the next row is tried (#204).
A computation that cannot be made to fit in 30 seconds is not run at all. A snapshot built before
pandas was added lacks it: rebuild with `scripts/sandbox-snapshot.mts` and update
`SANDBOX_SNAPSHOT_ID`.
**A run that could not be made settles its row too (2026-10-02).** When the code needs a
file that is not there, or prints `MISSING_DATA: <what>` (which the code-writer is told to
do, with a non-zero exit, when the goal needs data the project does not hold), the step is
`blocked` / `data_missing` at any exit code — never `code_executed` — and the named row
becomes **Not run** with that reason, "Recorded by PromptMaster: the run could not be
made". The sentinel is an instruction to a model: code that reports missing data in other
words and exits cleanly is still recorded as executed, and its row as Completed. In the
table, picking a status that needs a reason offers the row's own "Deviation" or "What
actually happened" text as the reason (`reasonFrom`), marked as filled in from the row;
no model reads it.

Removing a file does not touch runs already recorded. Limits: 30 s per command, 10 runs per Go
run (`SANDBOX_MAX_PER_RUN`), 600 s of execution per user per UTC day
(`SANDBOX_DAILY_SECONDS`), 5 output files of ≤1 MB each. Without
`SANDBOX_SNAPSHOT_ID` each run installs the packages first (~20–25 s wall time).
Cost is recorded only when `SANDBOX_USD_PER_VCPU_SECOND` is set, and is otherwise
null ("unknown"), never $0. `SANDBOX_ENABLED` must be `true` in production; when
it is not, every computation is honestly blocked on a missing tool.

### L-B4 — Go mode runs while the tab is open

The Go loop is browser-driven. Closing the tab pauses a run; reopening the project resumes
it, and whatever step was in flight is recorded as `interrupted` and never repeated. Two
tabs cannot drive one run (a lease with a 10 s heartbeat; the second tab watches and takes
over if the first goes quiet for 25 s). Other limits: the budget is a **window** of steps,
not dollars (`agent_runs.budget_usd` exists but is not enforced yet; model spend is in
`model_usage` as usual) — since B4 (2026-09-29) a used-up window offers "Continue for N
more steps", which starts a new run chained by `continues_run_id` under the same
authorization with a further `decisions` row; the chain is the project-level loop. Since
2026-10-01 an **Autonomous** authorization may name up to three further windows the run
starts on its own; each is still a new run with its own `decisions` row
(`metadata.auto = true`). Since 20261010000000 the database counts them: such a window is
inserted with `agent_runs.auto_continued = true`, and the run guard refuses one once the
chain already holds as many consecutive self-started windows as the authorization's
`auto_continue_windows` allows (never more than three at first; since `20261014000000_agent_keep_going.sql` the
database ceiling is 20, the Autonomous "Keep going" setting);
a window the user clicks is unmarked and starts the count again. What the guard cannot do
is tell a click from a client that leaves the mark off — the row is written by the user's
own session, so this holds the loop to its terms rather than defending the user against
their own browser. It is also forgotten on reload (a reloaded run asks before its next
window), and it still needs the tab open — a run that survives a closed tab is not
built. Also since 2026-10-01 the planner is given **what the project has already
decided** (`lib/agent/memory.ts`: skips and overrides with their reasons, reopenings,
priority decisions, suggestions taken and turned down, the user's answers to Go's
questions), rebuilt from the event log, the decision trail and earlier windows' steps on
every move, and the steps of up to four earlier windows are reloaded with a run. It is
the twenty most recent such lines, not everything; it holds decisions, not the content of
the work, which reaches the planner as stage summaries and established figures. A stop the user can
clear is stored as `agent_runs.needs` and shown as "I need you to…" with the one button
that clears it (approve the outline, unblock, confirm a requirement, keep waiting,
continue, confirm a large drafting run); a question keeps its own prompt. Under
Autonomous, Go decides a review table's **routine** findings itself (severity minor or
moderate, an enum since B3) and stops for the **material** ones (major, or of unknown
severity — every finding written before B3 counts as material); Checkpoint pauses before
deciding; Guided always asks. Fact-check, runs, alternatives and validation tables are
outcome tables and are never decided by Go; `check_literature` was not offered between
2026-09-28 and 2026-10-01 (no tool was connected) and now looks the project's named works
up in OpenAlex (see L-C6) — on the Literature stage it saves the result, from any other
stage it only reports it; since 2026-10-02, given search words or no list of works, it
searches OpenAlex by topic instead (L-C6);
since 2026-10-01 `generate_outline` is offered on whichever stage holds the outline —
Book's Outline stage, or the drafting stage of a workflow whose outline is derived
(Research), where it is built from the stages already done with no model call; before
that a Research run reached Drafting with no move that led to prose;
since B2b (2026-09-28) Go has the stage's own moves — `generate_outline`,
`draft_sections`, `revise_sections`, `apply_findings` — performed by the same functions
the buttons call (`lib/outline/actions.ts`, `lib/jobs/sections.ts`,
`lib/workflow/apply-findings.ts`). Until 2026-10-01 "succeeded" meant the performer
reported a change; since then the project is read back after the step
(`readOutcomeProof` / `verifyOutcome`: the saved version exists and holds text, the
written sections hold text, the check left an evaluation, a stage move moved the
cursor) and a step whose change is not found is recorded as failed. If that read-back
itself fails, the step keeps its status with "Not confirmed" appended rather than being
failed on a guess. What stays the user's: approving an outline, unblocking a stage,
ticking a manual requirement, deciding on findings; Go stops and says so. On Research a
reasoning move is always on offer, so the stop for a required approval is not raised before
the planner is asked; since 2026-10-02, when the planner then answers "objective complete"
on a stage whose only open requirement is that approval, the run asks for the approval
with its button instead of ending on "the model thinks the work is done" (until then an
Autonomous Research run stopped that way at the first such stage). (The inline
"I need you to…" card is B4; a request settled row by row — findings, claims, runs — has
"Go to the table", which only brings the rows into view, and leaves Resume in place —
until 2026-09-29 every card hid it, and a run stopped for material findings could not
be continued at all). A stage with nothing to cite — Outline approval has no artifact —
is completed by a move the user approved (Guided, Checkpoint, or an approved Autonomous
step) exactly as the transition bar completes it; a move an Autonomous run makes on its
own must cite a version (`workflow_events_agent_authorized`) — unless the stage holds no
artifact at all, which the trigger checks itself (20261004000000); so Outline approval
completes under Autonomous too, and the planner is told a left-open stage is the user's to close. A drafting step waits for its section jobs for up to ten
minutes and is then recorded `interrupted` — the sections go on being written by cron,
and Resume waits again without spending a step.

### L-C6 — Literature: every generated work is a candidate (2026-10-01)

The Literature stage's works are recalled from the model's knowledge; nothing is searched
or fetched. Each generated row is stored as **Suggested by PromptMaster — not retrieved**
(the server sets it; a status the model writes is dropped), the stage says how many rows
are still in that state, and the checklist counts "candidate works identified" and "works
retrieved or verified" separately. **Retrieved by PromptMaster** is set by a lookup in
OpenAlex (`POST /api/agent/literature`, `promptmaster/literature.py`; the "Look up these
works" button, or Go's `check_literature`): the title inside the citation is searched, a
record counts as found only if at least 80% of its title's words are in the named work,
its title has three or more meaningful words, and its year is within one of any year
named. **What "Retrieved" means is narrow: a record with this title exists.** Nobody has
read the work, so it does not mean the row's "what it established" is right. Books, grey
literature and anything OpenAlex does not index come back "not found" even when real;
at most 20 works are looked up at a time; no API key is used, so the lookup shares
OpenAlex's public rate limit (set `OPENALEX_MAILTO` on the backend for the polite pool).
The lookup does not reach Book's fact-check claims, whose "source" is free text. **Verified by me** is the user's, with a DOI or link
field the model is never asked to fill. The "retrieved or verified" item is *optional*:
making it required would stop every Research project at stage two until a retrieval tool
exists. Applies to Research v5; projects on earlier versions keep the old stage until
upgraded.

**Topic search (2026-10-02).** Go's `check_literature` can also *find* works: given search
words by the planner (`query`), or when no works are listed yet (the project's objective
is then the query), it searches OpenAlex (`POST /api/agent/literature-search`) and, on
the Literature stage, adds up to 8 returned records as new **Retrieved by PromptMaster**
rows with their DOI. The limits: it is a keyword search of one index, in the index's own
relevance order — not a systematic review, and not a judgement that a work is relevant or
good; "what it established" and "how it bears on this question" are left **empty**,
because no abstract or full text is read; a record already listed (same DOI or title) is
not added again and the list never exceeds 15; from any other stage the records are
reported and nothing is saved; there is no button for it — only Go searches. Reading a
source, open web search and paywalled full text are not built. A model revision of
the stage ("Revise this stage", or applying a check's fixes) rewrites the rows, and rows
it rewrites go back to **Suggested by PromptMaster** — the same as for looked-up rows —
so fill in the empty fields by hand or through the chat's row changes, which keep them.

### L-C7 — Statuses the model may set (2026-10-01)

A generated row's status is kept only where the row's schema says the model may set it
(`modelMaySet` in `lib/workflow/stage-artifact.ts`, enforced in `promptmaster/stage.py`).
Today that is one value: a run may arrive as **Not run** with its reason, marked "Set by
PromptMaster", when the draft already knows it could not be executed. **Completed** and
**Deviated** are never the model's to set — they record what a person or a tool did
(a sandbox run that executed sets **Completed** on the row it carried out; see L-B3).
Alternatives tables allow none. Rows in versions saved before this change
are untouched.

**Validation statuses (2026-10-02).** A validation row is now one of *Independently
reproduced*, *Supported by prior evidence*, *Consistency check only*, *Not reproduced* or
*Not attempted*, each with a one-line meaning under the table. *Independently reproduced*
is the user's alone to choose: the model, Go's triage and a change proposed from the chat
are never offered it (`requiresExecution`; `proposableStatuses` in
`lib/workflow/stage-artifact.ts`). The draft may set *Not attempted* with its reason. A
row saved earlier as `reproduced` keeps that value, counts as resolved, and reads
"Reproduced — kind not recorded" until the user reclassifies it; it is not converted,
because nothing on record says which kind it was. Nothing checks that the status a user
picks matches what the row's text says was done, and a sandbox run cannot set a
validation status. There is no separate "outcome" (supported / mixed / contradicted)
column yet. The statuses live in code, so they apply to every pinned workflow version;
the Validation stage's own guidance text still says "reproduced" until the next Research
template version.

**Proposed statuses (2026-10-06).** Superseded in part by L-41: judgments the draft can
see in its own words are now *proposed* — see there.

### L-C9 — Research write-up: two forms, chosen at the outline (2026-10-01)

Research v6 offers its write-up as a **full research paper** (nine sections) or a
**short report** (five: the answer, what was looked at, what was found, what could change
this, what to do next), declared as data on the template (`derived_outline.compact`). The
form to start from is a word match on the project's audience and output format
(`default_when`), which is a guess: the user chooses on the outline, and Go builds the
outline in the starting form. Which form an outline is in is read from its section ids,
not stored. Switching form regenerates the outline; sections already written under the
other form's headings are kept as orphans to reattach, not rewritten. Section writing is
also told to "say each thing once", which is an instruction, not a check — nothing
detects repetition after the fact. Only the write-up changes: the thirteen stages are
the same in both forms.

Since 2026-10-01 (later) every section is also told to keep to any stated length, to hold a
section of a memo, brief or short report to roughly 150 to 300 words, and to report only
what the brief and prior context say was established — a run that was not carried out is
written up as not carried out. These are instructions to a model, not checks: the first
short report drafted on production ran to 2,673 words over four sections and described
patterns in data that had never been provided, and nothing in the product would have
stopped it. Nothing measures a section's length or compares its claims with the record.

### L-C8 — Established figures: quoted, not checked (2026-10-01)

When a stage is completed, its figures are read out of its own text once
(`POST /api/extract-figures`) and stored on its artifact (`artifacts.key_figures`) with the
version they came from. A value is kept only if the stage's text contains it character
for character, so the list cannot hold a number the model computed, rounded or invented.
Later stages are handed the list and told to use those exact values and to state any
disagreement as a discrepancy. What this is **not**: a check. Nothing compares a later
stage's numbers with the list after it is written, so a model can still ignore the
instruction; and nothing judges whether the earlier figure was right. Figures come only
from stages that are *done* and only while they are about the stage's head version — a
stage left open contributes none, and one edited after completion drops out until it is
completed again. Long-form stages (the drafted sections) are not read for figures. The
call is skipped when the text has no digits, costs one small model call otherwise, and
is given 12 seconds; if it fails or runs out, the stage completes without figures.
Since 2026-10-01 a clean sandbox run's labelled output lines (`label: value` or
`label = value`, the value holding a number; at most twelve) are added to the stage's
figures directly, marked as printed by that run, with no model reading them
(`figuresFromOutput`); they are kept when the stage's text is later re-read for figures.
They still reach later stages only once the stage is done, like every other figure, and a
line the code did not print in that shape is not picked up.

### L-C4 — Fact-check: nothing is verified by PromptMaster

The claim table's states are provenance first, decision second (C3, 2026-09-29). PromptMaster
sets **Candidate source — verify it yourself** when the model named where a claim could be
checked and **No source found** when it could not; both count as *undecided*, so "Every claim
has a status" still means every claim was looked at by the author. The author's decisions are
**Verified by me**, **Unverifiable** (with a reason) and **Remove** (with a reason). **Verified
by PromptMaster** exists in the schema for a tool that reads a source and confirms the claim,
and is never offered in the dropdown; nothing reads sources, so no claim carries it today, and
the model's "source" is what it inferred from the manuscript, not something it fetched. Since
2026-10-01 the named sources can be **looked up** ("Look up these sources", the same OpenAlex
title search as Literature — L-C6): a source that is found moves the row to **Source found by
PromptMaster — check it says this**, with the record's real title and DOI on the row. That is
a provenance state like the other two and still counts as *undecided*: the lookup establishes
that a work with that title exists, not that it supports the claim. A source that is not a
published work (the author's own data, a website, an interview) is simply not found, and the
result says so in those words rather than calling it misremembered; a row with no source
named ("none found", or the **No source found** state) is not searched at all. On Book
the lookup is the user's button only — Go's `check_literature` is offered on Research. The
backend maps whatever the model says onto these states — a model "verified" becomes a
candidate at most (`_claim_provenance` in `promptmaster/stage.py`). Book v5 carries the prompt
text; projects on v4 and earlier keep their old statuses and see the "Upgrade workflow" button.
Rows written before C3 with the old `verified` value read as "Verified by me", which is what
that click meant then too.

### L-C5 — Word and PDF export: plain fidelity, and the browser's own PDF

The Word export (C4, 2026-09-29) is built in the browser from the assembled manuscript
Markdown with the `docx` package, loaded on demand: title, chapter headings (one per page),
sub-headings, paragraphs and bullets. Tables, code blocks, images and footnotes arrive as
plain text. The PDF export is a print view of the manuscript alone
(`/projects/[id]/print`) with a chapter per page; the file itself comes from the browser's
"Save as PDF", so its fonts and margins are the browser's. Neither export includes the
stage-by-stage record — that is the Markdown document and the JSON record, as before.
Long-form stages now show the manuscript's saved snapshots as version pills (C6); a
snapshot is read there, not restored into the sections (L-25 stands).

### L-C3 — Conflict detection: what it sees, and what it costs

PM-24's check runs on **side-chat instructions**, on **combined recommendations** (the
apply preview), and since 2026-10-01 on a **side-chat action button's revision** and on
**Go mode's own revisions** (`revise_stage` with an instruction, and `apply_findings`):
Go stops with "which should take priority?", and the user's answer lets the next revision
on that stage through, carrying the answer. One function serves all of them
(`findInstructionConflicts`, `lib/workflow/conflict-trail.ts`). It does not run on row
changes from the chat (shown row by row before saving), on the stage-level refine tools,
which carry no free text, or on stage generation. It compares an instruction with the
objective, constraints and decisions; it does not compare one stage's content with
another's — a number in a later stage that contradicts an earlier one is not caught here. The rule-based half (`instruction-conflicts.ts`)
reads a short phrase list on FR-15's six axes, so it catches opposite directions ("expand"
against "under 300 words") but not paraphrase. That is the job of the model half, which costs
one small JSON call per instruction and fails open: if the call fails, the instruction is sent
with whatever conflicts the rules found. Answers are stored as accepted or dismissed
`recommendations` rows with a `decisions` row (category `conflict:*`), so `decisions_type_chk`
is not widened.

### L-29 · Workflows a user designs: three page kinds, no chapters · `accepted`

Since 2026-10-03, "Design a workflow" (`POST /api/generate-workflow`, then
`templateFromDesign` and `validateTemplate`) saves a user's own template to
`workflow_templates`. The row has `is_system = false` and a `custom_` key, and RLS
admits only those (`wft_insert_own`).

**What a generated stage can be.** Only a writing page, a list or a check table:

since #222, an exact number of sections. The objective check is given those counts and may
not overrule them. A limit is read only in the phrasings `measurable.ts` knows. #224 added
"capped at", "limited to", "200 words max" and "a 200-word limit", after the survey-brief
test showed "capped at 200 words" was never measured. A phrasing it does not know measures
nothing rather than guessing, so the model's reading stands for it.

- **Sections** are the headings at the top level of the text, not counting a lone title.
  A brief that uses bold lines or numbered paragraphs instead of headings is counted by
  those bold lines, or not at all.
- **Labelled assumptions count as words.** Headings and a trailing "Word count:" or
  "Notes:" line do not.
- **"Generic roles" and "no invented company details"** cannot be measured. The model
  assesses them, and when it cannot quote a passage that shows one is met, the record says
  "Not verified".

### L-77 — Applied instructions and the IDs a revision keeps (2026-10-12)

Since 12 Oct, an instruction the user applies with "Change it" goes into every
later prompt and check, under accepted facts and above the reviewed prompt and the
brief (`lib/workflow/applied-instructions.ts`, `project_context.instructions_block`).
Only the latest 12 go out, each up to 1,500 characters. Standing changes such as
"the permitted evidence is now S1–S7" are offered as new facts in the same step. They
are found by a small model call and kept only when quoted from the instruction, so
one the model misses is still carried as the instruction itself.

Go does not save a revision that brings back an ID the user's own change removed,
or that rewrites an ID in another form (D3 → D3-P). It asks instead
(`lib/workflow/id-changes.ts`). An ID is matched by its shape: one to three capital
letters and up to three digits, with an optional suffix. An item with no visible ID
is not protected this way; the prompt rule "keep the record" is all that covers it.

The objective check, the criterion check and the expert review now read the
project context up to 40,000 characters. Go's planner reads 12,000, because it runs

### L-78 — How a named part is found and measured; cut-off drafts (2026-10-12)

A limit on a part, such as "a summary of at most 100 words", is measured on that
part. It is found under a heading that names it. The part ends at the next heading at
its level or above, at a bold or "Label:" line, at a heading naming another part, at a
table, or at a numbered question. A title that names the subject is not the part. With
no heading, the part is the opening prose before the first heading, table or question.
The record quotes the first and last words it counted, so a wrong boundary can be
seen. A summary that uses a bold-only line as a sub-heading inside itself is cut short
at that line.

"A 250-word executive summary", "≤ 250 words" and "keep it to 250 words" are now
read as limits. A count made in code overrules the model only for the part it
measured.

A prose draft the model cut off is continued up to twice before it is saved. If it is
still cut off, it is saved so the user can see it, but it is not marked complete and
Go says where it stops. A prose stage may now write up to 12,000 tokens (4,096 before).
A table stage is not continued.

### L-81 — The repair loop: what a repair reopens, and when Go stops repairing (2026-10-13)

When Revision or Editing saves a copy of the manuscript before rewriting it, only the
finished stages *after* that stage are reopened. The stages between Drafting and it,
such as Continuity, gave it its findings and are not reopened by applying them. Until
13 Oct they were: Continuity's recheck reopened Revision, Revision's rewrite reopened
Continuity, and Drafting reached v7 in one run. The trade-off is that Continuity does
not re-read the revised manuscript on its own. A user who wants a fresh continuity
pass reopens Continuity, and a later edit to Drafting by hand still reopens it.

Every reopening records why and when, including completing a stage again on new
evidence and going back to an earlier stage. A stage's repairs are therefore always
counted, and after two that did not settle it Go stops with the stage, what is still
unresolved, what the last attempt did, and three buttons:
- "Try once more" allows one more repair. It is held in the open tab only, so it is
  lost on reload.
- "Keep it as it is now" dismisses that stage's recheck and no other.
- "Open it" shows the stage.

The same stop follows a loop of repairs. A repair of a findings table is told which
rows are decided, and a decided row it leaves out is put back exactly as it was
instead of the whole repair being refused. Under Autonomous with "Handle them for me",
the routine findings a repair raises are decided with it. Major or unrated ones are
still the user's (L-74).

Go's "What changed" note on a repaired document goes to the version's change summary
and is no longer saved inside the document. Versions saved before 13 Oct still carry
it at the top.

The planner can offer 2–4 one-click answers with a question. Whether it does is a
model judgement, and an open question still has only the text box.

### L-82 — Continuing from a reopened stage; delegated chat revisions (2026-10-13)

A reopened earlier stage offers "Apply my changes and let Go continue". If the stage's
requirements hold, it is completed on its new version, which reopens the work after it
for a recheck, and Go starts from where the project is. If they do not hold, Go is
asked to finish the stage while keeping the user's changes. That is a model revision of
the user's version, and the earlier versions stay in its history. The Go panel itself
still appears only on the project's current stage.

With "Routine decisions: handle them for me", a "Change it" proposal in the side chat
that changes no accepted fact is applied without a click. The notice says so and keeps
"Restore the previous version". A proposal that changes a fact still waits, because
recording a fact is the user's decision. The check after applying measures what can be
measured (lengths, sections, questions) on the saved text. It does not re-run a model
evaluation.

Pre-authorised further windows are read back from the record after a reload.

### L-83 — The form of the work, fiction in the fact-check, and each stage's mode (2026-10-13)

A screenplay, a stage play or fiction (novel, novella, short story) is recognised from
the objective, output format and constraints by a keyword list, not a model. On the
Book workflow:
- The sidebar says "Book workflow · Screenplay".
- The stage label and guidance say screenplay, sequence and script.
- Every prompt is told the form and how it is written: scene headings, action and
  dialogue for a screenplay, with no options left undecided.

The stage definitions themselves are unchanged, so an exported audit trail still names
the Book stages. A form the list does not know, such as a podcast script or a comic,
is treated as a book. "Fictional evidence" in a business brief is not read as fiction.

When the work is fiction, the fact-check prompt sorts what the draft says into four
kinds:
1. Invented story material. It gets no row unless it is presented as historical fact,
   and then "Fiction — not an external claim".
2. Internal continuity. Left out, because Continuity covers it.
3. Historical setting.
4. Claims about real dates, institutions and people.

Setting and real-world claims are checked, and an unresolved alternative in the draft
is left to Critique. Each claim row also carries a kind: real world, historical setting or invented. A row the model marks as invented is set to Fiction in code and never looked up. This was added because, on the first production run, the model listed invented memoranda and searches as claims despite the prompt telling it not to. The sorting is still a model judgement. A row it gets wrong can be set
by hand, and "Fiction" settles a row without a reason. "No source found yet" is a

A stage that recommends a mode is now worked in it (Critique as Cold Critic), by the

### L-87 — Part limits, kept lines, invented details and one conflict question (2026-10-14)

A word limit written about one part ("Executive update must be no more than 100 words",
"Section 1 (executive update) is limited to 100 words") is measured on that part's
section alone, found by its heading or, for "Section N", by position. A part whose
heading is not in the text is not measured at all, rather than measured as the whole
deliverable. Wording the parser does not recognise still measures nothing. A repair of
a part's limit revises that part and puts every other section back in code.

A revision is compared with the version it was made from, line by line, in code. A line
counts as kept when it is there word for word, with only its numbers or times changed,
or, for a longer line, with three quarters of its words. Short lines it dropped without
being asked are put back where they stood. Longer passages are only reported, because a
paragraph rewritten past recognition would otherwise appear twice. Nothing is checked
when the instruction asks for a rewrite or a removal, or when a repair is shortening to
meet a measured limit; then what goes is taken to be what was asked. A line that states a
value a fact has since replaced is not put back. The matching is word-based: a line moved
into a merged paragraph counts as kept, and a line reworded with new words counts as gone.

When the brief, an applied instruction or a requirement says not to invent details,
every prompt carries a fact boundary, and each revision is checked by one small model
call for personal details nobody supplied. The check is the model's judgement; it is held
in code only to quotes that are verbatim in the new text and absent from the old text and
from what the user supplied. It can miss a detail and can flag a plain restyle. A
revision it flags is not applied without a click. A failed check reports nothing.

An instruction that conflicts with something is asked about once: the lead conflict,
with a recorded approval first, its explanation, what keeping the record means, and two
short labels. Any others are folded, set to their recommended side. Keeping a recorded
approval is recommended; overriding it asks once more. Whether a fact is an approval
rule is read by the model (`protects_approval`), not by a rule in code. An "as of" date,
a recorded request and a repeated decision are told not to be conflicts in the prompt,
and applied chat instructions are no longer counted as pending, in code.

### L-88 — Facts recorded once, the change sequence, history in repairs, and actionable blockers (2026-10-15)

Facts from the side chat are compared with the facts on record before they are offered
and again before they are inserted, by their words and figures with punctuation and case
ignored. A fact reworded with new words is a new fact. Filler ("all other facts remain
unchanged") and instructions about the request ("Before recording anything…") are
dropped by pattern in code and by the prompt; a form the patterns do not know relies on
the prompt. A statement naming someone's request is never stored as a requirement.

The "still states" check after a fact change treats as history: lines under a heading
that names a change or decision log, history or superseded values; tables headed that
way; quoted text; sentences about a request, proposal, decline or version; and a date
that only ends a task window. A stale value written in such a place is not caught. On a
table stage only undecided rows are checked.

A blocker's threshold ("$12 per person or less: $240 / 20") is found by the model. Code
removes any figure that is neither on the record nor one arithmetic step from two
figures on it; it does not find a threshold the model missed. "Updated and checked" is
shown for twelve hours after the last stage a change reopened is finished again, or
until dismissed. The objective check sets aside a "not met" that restates a recorded
request by word overlap; a request paraphrased with other words relies on the prompt.

### L-89 — Later authorised changes, approver names, outside revisions and one pilot (2026-10-17)

A conflict recommends applying the new instruction when the instruction itself records
the approval or a confirmed amendment. The model proposes the words; code accepts them
only if they are copied from the instruction, say approved, confirmed, authorised or
signed off, do not relay a request ("says", "treat as", "proposes", "requests",
"pending"…), and name only people the project already knows. A relayed approval worded
without one of those verbs, or a stranger with a name already on record, relies on the
prompt. Dates are not compared: the instruction is taken as the user's latest word,
unless the brief asks for a frozen historical report.

The approver check flags a person named as approving something when the brief, context
and facts never name that person as approving anything. It checks the name, not the
item: if Elena approved the November change, it cannot tell whether she also approved
the baseline. How history is worded (superseded, corrected, declined) is a prompt rule.

A revision made in another assistant from an older export is now detected, if its stamp
survives (18 Oct). Every Markdown export stamps each stage's head version
(`[PromptMaster output v5 1a2b3c4d]`), and the Word, PDF, LaTeX and notebook exports
stamp the deliverable. When text pasted into "Change it" for the whole document carries
an older stamp for that stage, only the user's own edits against that version (a line
diff) are applied to the current one, and the proposal says so. Limits:
- the other assistant must keep the stamp. Without it the paste is treated as before,
  drafted against the current version with no warning;
- the edits are applied by the model as an instruction, so an edit to a passage that
  changed here since can still be resolved the wrong way;
- a long-form manuscript is not stamped: it is revised chapter by chapter.

Fixed in #290: when an accepted fact is dated later than the brief's "as of", code adds a CURRENT REPORTING DATE line to the facts block. The brief's value for an item a later fact updates is the baseline. ### L-91 — Section limits, length repairs, the row guard and one verdict (2026-10-19)

Word and item limits are read per section from the brief: the name before "(≤N words)" or
"at most N words" in a list of deliverables. A limit with no name applies to the whole
text only when its sentence says so ("in total", "overall", "the package"). A fact saying
"no N-word limit on …" retires the matching limit. A section whose name does not appear in
any heading of the draft is not measured at all (it is left to the model's check), so a
draft that renames a section escapes its limit.

After a length repair, code checks only that no heading disappeared and that a section
with two or more timings kept some. Other required content (an exercise, a named
activity without a heading) relies on the prompt.

The row guard protects rows that the user or the policy decided. A model's provenance
label is not a decision. A row under a new id counts as carried when it keeps at least
70% of its words and the same decision. A heavy rewrite of a decided row is therefore
refused even if the meaning survives. Refused revisions are stored from 19 October. The
Theory of Everything rows `i7` and `i8` were refused before then, so whether they were
really lost cannot be shown.

Go's gate, the stage bar and the next step read one verdict (`check-verdict.ts`). That
verdict is the evaluator's completeness judgment, so it is model-judged. A complaint that
is only about format, on a table stage, does not block. Next steps written inside
fictional or sample sections are dropped by matching their words to those sections. A
next step paraphrased from the exercise can still be listed.

When Go returns to an earlier stage to apply a later review stage's rows, those rows go
with the instruction, up to 6,000 characters (#311). The objective check's rule that work
already saved in the project is never a blocker is a prompt rule.

### L-92 — Approvals, brief-only corrections and assumed facts (2026-10-19)

An approval verb ("authorizes", "approves", "signs off", "authorized") by a named
authority makes a statement an approval. "Has not approved", "pending approval" and "asks
the board to approve" do not. The model labels the rest. An approval worded without one
of those verbs relies on the model. Whether the named authority may approve that item
(the CEO within staff capacity, the board for capital) is not checked in code.

A correction to a figure stated only in the brief now names what it replaces, when its
wording says so: ", superseding the earlier $30 million", ", not $30 million",
"corrected from". It still has no `supersedes` link, because there is no earlier fact,
and a correction worded without one of those phrases is not paired.

`origin` marks a fact as `model_assumed` when its figures, or most of its words, are not
in what the user wrote. A close paraphrase counts as stated. Facts recorded before
19 October are all `user_stated` except policy ones.

### L-93 — Conclusions that depend on a changed fact; conflicts with a request (2026-10-19)

When a fact replaces something, one model call per prose-stage check or repair lists the
sentences whose conclusion no longer follows (`dependent_claims.py`). Code keeps only
quotes found word for word, and fails a repair that leaves one word for word. Which
sentences are flagged is model-judged; a stale conclusion the call misses is not caught,
and a reworded but still stale sentence passes. Item stages (tables) are not checked this
way.

A conflict with the accepted record is kept without asking when routine decisions are
"handle them for me" and the conflict is a request with no authority behind it
(`lib/workflow/routine-conflicts.ts`, decided in code):
- In the side chat and its action buttons: the conflicting words only report someone
  else's request ("The COO asks purchasing to order…"). The model proposes those words;
  code keeps the flag only if they are copied from the instruction, read as a request and
  not an approval or first-person wording, and come from nobody the brief, facts or
  decisions say "may approve" or "may authorise" (`conflicts.py` `_request`).
- In Go (Autonomous only): any revision or apply Go chose for itself.

The record is kept, the choice is recorded on the decision trail as the policy's
(`policy: 'routine_decisions'`), the revision goes ahead within it, and a note with
"Apply it anyway" is shown (side chat note; Go step). "Apply it anyway" asks the usual
question.

Still asked: routine decisions set to "Ask me"; Go under Guided or Checkpoint; an
instruction that records an approval; the user's own instruction; two instructions in
conflict; a request from someone the record says may approve (a change of authority or
scope); a conflict found only by the keyword rule. Which words are a request is the
model's proposal; a request it fails to quote is asked about, never kept silently.

Positive findings ("Accept", "No change required") are never counted as unmet, and the
unmet wording is matched only on a finding's own text. A stage with open findings shows
"done · N open" on the rail; it still counts as done.

### L-96 — Go's round limit is fixed, and new material is judged by timestamps (2026-10-20)

Under Autonomous + "Handle them for me", Go starts at most two rounds of an ongoing workflow
after the last outside input, then moves on to the stage after the round (`round-close.ts`).
It counts the following as outside input:
- a data file;
- a fact recorded from the brief, chat, a file or the user's edit;
- a round the user started themselves.

Limits of this rule:
- **The two-round limit is fixed.** It is not a setting.
- **Pasted text is not counted.** Text pasted into the brief without being recorded as a fact
  does not count as new material.
- **Exploration is affected too.** An exploration under the same policy also stops looping
  after two rounds and moves to the write-up. The write-up's approval is still the user's,
  and so is any further round.

**The objective check's history and limitations (same day, H1b–c).**

The check is now told which saved versions exist. It is also given the earlier versions
the text cites, such as "Check v18" (a table's rows with their decisions; prose clipped to
3,000 characters; 12,000 in all).

Limits:
- **Citation form.** A citation is recognised only in the form "<stage label> v<N>". A
  version cited another way ("the eighteenth check") is not found.
- **Limitation allowances.** A gap becomes a limitation only when the brief, the context or
  the accepted facts contain an allowance that code can find. That means "limitation",
  "may remain", "left open" or "note what is unknown". Other wordings stay blockers.
- **Saved-work decisions.** A `needs_decision` about a finding on a saved version is moved to
  "Proposed, not done" as "Settle from the saved record". Nothing then settles it
  automatically. The write-up stage sees it only through the stages it reads.
- **Not passed to the write-up.** The package stage is not handed the limitations list. It
  carries gaps through its own prompt ("Information gaps and outstanding requests").

### L-97 — Web access: what it does and does not do (2026-10-20)

Sean asked (20 Oct) for web search with citations and saved sources, and an on/off button. Each
project has a Web access switch, on by default since 22 Oct (off before; the migration turned
on every project nobody had switched). Web search and Go's search_web move run only
while it is on. Limits:
- **The backend does not read the switch.** It is stateless (CLAUDE.md), so it trusts the
  `web_access` the browser sends and refuses "off". The database is the real guard: a source
  cannot be saved unless the project's switch is on (insert policy). A caller who skipped the
  browser could still pay for a search, but could not save its results.
- **Excerpts, not pages.** A source is what the search engine returned from the page (up to 600
  characters), not the page itself. Nothing fetches or reads the full page, a PDF behind it, or
  a paywalled article. A stage that cites [S1] is citing that excerpt.
- **One engine.** Exa, through OpenRouter, at about $0.007 per search plus the tokens. It finds
  what Exa indexes; recency and coverage are Exa's.
- **Citations are checked only for existence.** Code removes a citation to a source that is not
  on record ([S7] with five saved). It does not check that [S2] supports the sentence it follows.
  That is the model's reading of the excerpt.
- **Go searches only from stages that write.** It doesn't search on review tables or on the stage
  that closes a round.
- **The side chat decides for itself (22 Oct).** While Web access is on, every Ask message costs
  one more small call, which decides whether the question needs the web; a search follows only
  when it does. That decision is a model's judgement: a question it wrongly thinks the project
  answers is answered without searching; asking it to "search for …" or "look up …" does
  search. The chat is told on every message whether it can search, so it never denies it. Change it (Instruct) never searches; it cites the
  saved sources. A failed search doesn't lose the reply: it is answered from the project and
  says so. What the chat cites is saved before the reply is, numbered after the saved sources;
  past 40 saved sources (the most a request carries) the numbers stop lining up.
- **On by default costs something on every project (22 Oct).** Since the switch starts on, every
  Ask message in the side chat makes the extra decision call above unless the user turns Web
  access off, and Go may search from any stage that writes. The Web sources panel stays folded
  until a source is saved, so the switch is one click further away than the heading's "web
  access on".

### L-100 — Every writer may search the web; checks and setup do not (2026-10-22)

"Every AI agent should have web access." While a project's Web access is on (the default), these
search the public web first when the work needs current or outside facts:
- stage drafts and regenerations
- Change it and "Save this discussion as a new version"
- Continue writing, and the Refine, Challenge, Reframe and Drift tools
- applying recommendations or findings, and the outline
- Go's drafts, rechecks, reasoning moves and expert packages
- the side chat and the start-screen conversation

All of them use one helper (`web_search.with_web`), and what it finds is saved and cited as [S n],
the same way the side chat's sources are.
- **One extra call, every time.** Each of these makes one small model call to decide whether a
  search is needed, even when it is not. A search, when made, is a second call plus OpenRouter's
  search fee. Both are metered in `model_usage`. Turning Web access off removes both.
- **Checks never search.** Evaluations, conflict and claim checks, fact and figure extraction,
  dependent-claim checks, objective assessment and reply-action parsing judge what the project
  holds. A check that pulls in outside facts no longer checks the project.
- **Setup does not search.** Guide me, the workflow designer and the setup suggestion run
  before a project exists and design the work rather than state facts. The start-screen
  conversation does search, and its sources come with the project.
- **Chapters do not search yet.** Long-form sections are written by the server-side job
  (`lib/jobs/generator.ts`), which has no path to save sources. They cite the sources already
  saved.
- **A retry does not search again.** When Go retries a recheck that left an old value behind,
  only the first attempt may search.
- **Numbering assumes one writer at a time.** New sources are numbered after the ones the
  request carried, as `saveSources` appends them. Two writers searching on the same project at
  once (a stage draft and the side chat, say) can number a source differently from where it is
  saved. A project sends at most 40 current sources, so the same holds past 40.

### L-101 — Output language: what follows it and what does not (2026-10-23)

"Can we do pick your language?" A project has a **Writes in** language (`projects.language`;
English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, German, Italian or Dutch), chosen on the start screen
and in the project setup. The browser sends it as `X-PromptMaster-Language`, and
`llm_client._with_language` adds one directive to the system message of **every** model call
that request makes: drafts, checks, the side chat, Go, and background chapters (the drain sends
the language saved with the job).
- **Work already written stays as it is.** Changing the language mid-project changes what is
  written next. Earlier stages, saved facts and sources keep their language, and are quoted as
  written.
- **Quotes, figures and fixed values are not translated.** Figures and quotations are copied
  verbatim, so a check's figure match still works. JSON keys and enumerated values (High/Low,
  statuses) stay English, which the code reads.
- **Some rules read the user's own words in English only.** The "don't invent details" rule and
  the format-only check reason are read in all seven languages. Word limits ("at most 500
  words"), the as-of date rule, and measurable requirements are read in English only; said in
  another language, they are left to the model rather than enforced.
- **Only languages with spaces between words.** Word counts and section measures count words
  by spaces, so Chinese, Japanese and Thai are not offered.
- **The interface's own words are English** until the interface translation (Phase 5) ships.

### L-102 — Folders: one owner, three levels, one folder per project (2026-10-23)

"Organizing projects and folders ... however someone wants to freely organize." Projects
can be filed in folders from the list's sidebar (drag on a computer, or the card's folder
button anywhere).
- **Folders are the user's own.** There is no sharing and no team folder; that waits for
  organization accounts (Exhibit B, Stage 3).
- **At most three levels deep.** The database refuses a deeper or circular tree.
- **One folder per project.** A project is filed in one folder or none (Unfiled), not
  tagged into several.
- **Order is by name.** Folders sort by name; there is no hand ordering yet, although
  the table has a `position` for it.
- **Deleting a folder deletes no project.** Its projects become Unfiled and the folders
  inside it move to the top level (not into its own parent).
- **The list still shows 50 at a time.** The folder filter runs on the server, so a
  folder's projects are found even when they are older than the newest 50 overall.

### L-104 — Using another project: text and facts, read when the project opens (2026-10-23)

"The project you are working on can use that information from those other chosen files or
projects." A project can draw on up to five of the user's other projects (Other projects,
on every stage; or "Use in another project" on a card in the list). Each brings its
deliverable as it stands, meaning the head version or the manuscript's written chapters,
plus its current facts unless that is turned off. Every stage, check, chat and Go step is
sent them as [R1]…, ranked below this project's own objective, facts, instructions and
constraints (`precedence.py`).
- **Text, not files.** A referenced project's data files and images are not copied across.
  Code this project runs sees only its own `/data`.
- **Read when the project opens.** An edit to the referenced project in another tab reaches
  this one on the next open or reload.
- **One hop.** A referenced project's own references are not followed.
- **Capped.** 16,000 characters of each and 40,000 in all, shared evenly and marked where
  cut. Go's planner and the checks that take a short context see the title and the first
  500 characters only.
- **Its facts are not this project's.** They are cited with [R n] and are never treated as
  accepted here. A figure or detail quoted from a reference counts as supplied in the
  figure and new-claim checks.
- **In the trash means unused.** A soft-deleted project is shown as in the trash and sent
  nowhere until restored. Purging it removes the reference.
- **The list's "Use in another project" offers the projects shown.** When a folder is
  selected, only that folder's projects are offered as targets.


### L-105 — Another project beside this one: what the split view does (2026-10-23)

"Dual projects at the same time side-by-side like split screen and you can integrate with each
other." **Open another project** (in the workspace header, or **Open beside** on a project in
*Other projects*) shows a second project in the side chat's slot, as a **Reading** tab beside
**Side chat**.
- **Read-only.** The pane loads its own copy of the other project (each stage's head version
  only) and writes nothing to it. To change that project, open it.
- **Two ways across, both the user's choice.** **Send this section to the side chat** puts the
  section, quoted and with its source, into this project's chat composer, unsent.
  **Use as reference in this project** adds it to *Other projects* (L-104), so every stage,
  check and chat here draws on it.
- **One other project at a time, on a tablet or wider.** The slot holds the chat or the reader,
  not both at once: three columns do not fit beside a 720px page. On a phone the entry points
  are hidden and the slot is the chat only.
- **As of when it was opened.** Edits made to the other project in another tab show after it is
  closed and opened again (or the page reloads).
- The choice is kept for this browser tab (session storage), per project.
